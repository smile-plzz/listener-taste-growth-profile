# Research notes

Running log — hypotheses, open questions, and sources still to check. Not part of the paper itself.

## Confirmed via initial literature search (2026-08-06)

- Reminiscence bump for music: adults disproportionately prefer/remember music from adolescence–mid
  20s. Original finding: Holbrook & Schindler (1989), peak ~23.5. Replicated/extended: Jakubowski et
  al. (2020, cross-sectional).
- Gender difference in bump timing: men peak earlier (~16), women later (~19+) — per Psychology
  Today coverage citing the underlying research; **the primary source for this specific claim still
  needs to be pinned down and cited directly**, not just via secondary coverage.
- Kalia (2015, Spotify/Echo Nest data): active new-music discovery rises through teens, declines
  through 20s, stabilizes ~age 33. Faster decline for men than women. Having children predicts a
  sharp drop in mainstream listening independent of age.

## Not yet integrated

- Rentfrow & Gosling's MUSIC model (2003) and later revisions — personality-correlate structure of
  music preference. Relevant to *why* certain genres cluster by life stage/personality, not just
  *when* taste stabilizes. Need a fresh literature pass before citing specifics.
- Openness-to-experience (Big Five) trajectories across age and their correlation with musical
  openness specifically — flagged in search results (streaming access broadening older listeners'
  range) but not yet chased to a primary source.
- Cross-cultural / cross-genre generalizability: nearly all located sources are Western
  (US/UK)-listener-based. Unclear how much of this generalizes globally — worth flagging as a
  limitation in the paper rather than assuming universality.

## Hypotheses to test empirically (see draft.md §5)

1. Life-stage variables (partnering, parenthood, job change) predict discovery-rate decline better
   than calendar age alone.
2. The reminiscence-bump window is stable across generations (i.e., a 60-year-old's bump and a
   25-year-old's future bump will land in the same *relative* life period, teens–mid-20s), rather
   than shifting with each generation's media environment.
3. Genre/era diversity (measured as entropy of a listener's play distribution) narrows with age but
   plateaus rather than continuing to shrink indefinitely.
