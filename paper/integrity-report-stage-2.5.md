## Phase B — Citation context (does the source say what the draft says it says?)

**B-1 [HIGH] — §14 misstates the hip-hop figure's construct and its age bracket, and misattributes
its origin.** The draft's table reads: "54% of 20–24-year-olds say they *generally like* it, vs. 5%
of over-55s," sourced in the reference list as "Nielsen data via headphonesaddict.com."

The source page states that 54% of 20–24s named hip-hop/rap their **favorite** genre — a materially
stronger claim than "generally like" — against 5% in the **55–64** bracket specifically, not all
over-55s. The page attributes the figure to **Statista**, undated; **Nielsen appears nowhere on it.**

Three corrections in one row: the dependent variable, the age bracket, and the upstream attribution.
The bracket error is the mild one; the "generally like" / "favorite" swap makes the figure look like
weaker evidence for a broader claim than it actually is, and the Nielsen attribution lends the number
an authority its real provenance does not support.

**B-2 [MEDIUM] — The gender moderator in §5.1 rests on a source this pass could not open.**
The claim that discovery declines faster for men than for women is attributed to Kalia (2015). The
statsignificant.com re-analysis, which the draft names as corroboration, **corroborates the age
figure but contains no gender analysis at all** — it gives Deezer's 31 and Spotify's 33, and confirms
the parenthood effect, and is silent on gender. So the gender claim currently has one unreachable
source and no corroboration, while §5.1 presents its convergence with §4.2 as "one of the more robust
things in this review." That characterization is not currently supported. Either restore access to
Kalia and quote it directly, or downgrade the claim.

**B-3 [LOW] — Holbrook & Schindler peak figure.** The draft states the peak "at around age 23.5."
The 1989 paper's own language is a peak "in about the 24th year." Both numbers circulate; the draft's
decimal implies a precision the abstract does not state. Either cite 23.5 to the specific table it
comes from, or match the source's phrasing.

**B-4 — Verified correct in context** (no action): Metallica "Master of Puppets" Hot 100 debut at
**No. 40**, ~17.5M weekly US streams (the draft's "~650% streaming increase" traces to Variety;
Billboard separately reports ~400% daily growth — both figures exist, so the draft should say which
measure it means); Fleetwood Mac "Dreams" re-entry at **No. 21**, peak **No. 12**, best streaming
week (13.4M streams); Kate Bush "Running Up That Hill" UK No. 1, **37 years** after release, oldest
female artist to top the UK chart; Jakubowski et al.'s methodology-change argument; Chee et al.'s
partial replication; Bonneville-Roussy et al.'s 250k+ sample and trend directions; the LFM-2b dataset
properties underwriting §18.

## Phase C — Statistical and derived data

**C-1 [PASS] — §14.5 null-model arithmetic is internally correct.** Spot-checked at four rows
against `age = (Y − P) + 15 … (Y − P) + 24` with Y = 2026: 1959→76–91 ✓ (the low bound uses P=1959
and the high bound P=1961 — the row is a peak *range*, so the derived range compounds both, applied
consistently throughout); 1986–1991→50–64 ✓; 2010–2015→26–40 ✓; 2021–2025→16–29 ✓. The rows are
consistent with each other and with the stated formula.

**C-2 [MEDIUM] — Scope tension: two of the paper's most distinctive sections are original
derivations inside a paper that states it "collects no primary data" (§2.1).** §14.5's null model,
§14.6's four deviation classes, and §14.7's attribute profiles are the author's own analytical
contributions, not synthesis. §14.7 labels itself honestly ("the framework's predicted attribute
profile… not a finding") and §14.5 says "every age range in it is a derivation, not a measurement" —
so the sections do not misrepresent themselves. But §2.1's blanket statement and §1's Contribution
paragraph ("the contribution is the *decomposition*") both undersell what §14.5–§14.7 actually do. A
reviewer will read this as either false modesty or a scope inconsistency. Resolve in §2.1 by
distinguishing *no primary data collection* from *original derivation from published parameters*.

**C-3 [LOW] — §14's remaining Tier D figures are unverified.** The country (56% 45+, 62% female),
classical (29% under 35), rock (20.5% of 2022 US album sales), prog (75%/63%) and Warped Tour (~90%
15–25) figures were not traced to primary sources this pass. The draft's own caveat closing §14
already states that none report sampling methodology. Given B-1 — where the one figure that *was*
traced turned out to be misdescribed in three ways — the rest of the row should be treated as
suspect until traced, not merely as low-tier.
